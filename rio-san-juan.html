The short version

How to get to Río San Juan, in 30 seconds

Río San Juan is a small town at the eastern end of the Dominican north coast, in the province of María Trinidad Sánchez. It is known for the Gri-Gri lagoon, a mangrove channel that opens onto the sea at the edge of town, and for the beaches just beyond it — Playa Caletón, Playa Grande and Playa Preciosa — which are backed by cliffs and forest rather than hotels. The closest airport is Gregorio Luperón International (POP). SG Caribbean Transfers & Tours runs private door-to-door transfers from POP at a fixed US$98.50 for 1 to 6 guests, with no prepayment: you pay your driver in cash when you arrive.

Once you are here

What is within reach of Río San Juan

Río San Juan is a working town, not a resort, and that is the appeal: the beaches nearby have cliffs and forest behind them instead of hotels. These are the places our guests actually ask us to take them to.

Laguna Gri-Gri

Mangrove lagoon

A channel of dark green water running out through the mangroves to the sea, right at the edge of town. Small boats take you down it and out along the cliffs to a swimming cove. It is the thing Río San Juan is known for and it takes a morning.

Playa Grande

Atlantic beach

A long, wide beach of pale sand with forested cliffs behind it and real Atlantic surf in front. Nothing gentle about it on a big day — but on a calm one it is the most impressive beach on this coast, and there is a golf course on the headland above.

Playa Caletón

Sheltered cove

The small one, tucked into a cove between the town and Playa Grande: calm, shallow, turquoise, with a handful of fish shacks on the sand. Where you go when Playa Grande is too rough to swim.

Playa Preciosa

Wild beach

Next along from Playa Grande and emptier still — a wide arc of sand with strong water and almost nothing built on it. Beautiful to walk, and one to ask a local about before swimming.

Cabarete

Along the coast

Back west along the coast road: the kitesurfing town, and the nearest place with a real night out. See the full Cabarete guide.

Cabrera

Clifftop town

Further east again, a village on the cliffs with viewpoints straight down onto the Atlantic and a couple of hidden coves below. The end of the north coast before the road turns inland towards Samaná.
