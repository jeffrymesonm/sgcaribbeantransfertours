The short version

How to get to Cabarete, in 30 seconds

Cabarete is a beach town on the north coast of the Dominican Republic, in the province of Puerto Plata, sitting on the coastal highway just east of Sosúa. It is built along one street between the sand and the hills, and it is known worldwide for kitesurfing, windsurfing and surfing. The closest airport is Gregorio Luperón International (POP). SG Caribbean Transfers & Tours runs private door-to-door transfers from POP to Cabarete at a fixed US$33.95 for 1 to 6 guests, boards included, with no prepayment: you pay your driver in cash when you arrive.

Once you are here

What is within reach of Cabarete

Cabarete is one long street between the beach and the hills, which makes it walkable and keeps everything else close. These are the places our guests actually ask us to take them to.

Kite Beach

Kitesurfing

Just west of town, and the reason Cabarete is on the world map. The bay funnels the trade wind straight across the water, the wind builds through the day, and by the afternoon the sky is full of kites. Worth going even if you never leave the sand.

Playa Encuentro

Surf break

The surf break of the north coast, on the road between Cabarete and Sosúa. Mornings are the session — the water is cleanest and the wind has not arrived yet. Several surf schools work the beach if you have never stood up before.

Cabarete Bay

In town

The town beach itself: windsurfers in the afternoon, and a row of restaurants that put their tables directly on the sand once the sun goes down. Everything Cabarete does in the evening happens along this strip.

El Choco National Park & the caves

Nature reserve

Behind the town, a lagoon and a set of limestone caves with clear pools you can swim in, inside a protected reserve of forest and birdlife. It is the standard plan for a day with no wind, and cool enough to be a relief at midday.

Sosúa

Neighbouring town

The calm alternative, the next town west along the coast road: a reef-sheltered horseshoe bay where you can snorkel straight from the sand, plus the small museum that tells how the town was founded by Jewish refugees in 1940. See the full Sosúa guide.

Charcos de Damajagua

Full-day excursion

Limestone pools you climb up and then jump and slide back down. The single most requested excursion from this coast, and the one people talk about for years afterwards.
